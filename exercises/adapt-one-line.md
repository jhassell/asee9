# Adapt one line

Six minutes. You leave with one change to one course, written down.

After [`hands-on.md`](hands-on.md), write four lines:

1. The rule you gave the agent, and what changed: ____
2. One claim you checked, and the evidence you used: ____ In MY field, I would check against: ____
3. One thing no automated check could prove: ____
4. Your course: what will the agent do, what evidence must students hand in, and one rubric criterion with its weight: ____

Now line 4. Start with one line you will add to one assignment. Pick one, or
write your own:

- "Your demonstration must include a rollback story: something that went wrong
  and how you recovered."
- "Keep a delegation log: which assistant you used, the key prompts, one
  mistake it made, and how you caught it."
- "Every prompt edit gets a changelog entry: what changed, what you expected,
  what you observed. 'Made it better' is not an entry."
- "Every source you cite must be quoted, and the quote must be findable in the
  source."

Then write, on line 4:

- **Your course.** Name it.
- **What will the agent do?** If students only use a chat assistant, write what
  they ask it to do.
- **What evidence must students hand in?** Something you can open: a
  transcript, a log of each step, a test result, or a file.
- **One rubric criterion that grades your line, with its weight.** Example:
  "Rollback story is specific and recoverable: 10 points."

Then show it to a colleague and ask one question: *how would a student game
this?* Fix your line if they found a way.

The changelog line has a full version, with grading levels: `PROMPTS.md` in
[`starter-assignment.md`](starter-assignment.md), graded by row 4 of
[`rubric-template.md`](rubric-template.md). The rollback line goes with the
**Recovery** row in [`rubric-template.md`](rubric-template.md) and with
[`starter-build-agent.md`](starter-build-agent.md).
